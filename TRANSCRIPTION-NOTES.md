## Richard Guise — two accounts, neither settled

His record now holds both openly, side by side, rather than picking one. The
site asserts neither; `wouldSettleIt` names the evidence that would decide it.

One thing to be clear about: the **Richard Guise of Clewer** in the Gillian D.R.
research is a *different man*. Cambridge BMus 1758, lay vicar at St George's
Windsor, choirmaster at Westminster Abbey, married Elizabeth Windham 1761, died
1806 and buried in the Abbey — he never came to New South Wales and was buried
fifteen years before ours died. That line is a possible wider family, not an
identity, and the record says so.

It is also worth holding both lightly because they may not conflict: Guise is a
town in Picardy, and the Gloucestershire Guises are a Norman family — de Guise —
who came to England centuries earlier.

## Richard Guise — the French account, from the Bell family database

The chart's header reads *Sergeant Richard Guise, b 1757, d 9-4-1821*. The Bell
family database entry (bellsite.id.au, P16607) fills that out:

- Born **1757 at Lorraine, France**. Fled France during the Revolution.
- **14 October 1789** joined the Royal Grenadier Horse Guards in England.
- **1792**, landed at Sydney aboard the **Atlantic**, a corporal in the **102nd
  Regiment — the New South Wales Corps**, the "Rum Corps". Later sergeant, then
  grazier.
- Died **16 April 1821** at Liverpool, NSW, aged 64, of a liver complaint. The
  chart says 9-4-1821; the two disagree by a week.

So he was **French, but not First Fleet** — the Atlantic brought him out in
1792, four years after the First Fleet. The site now says so.

Two more from the same source:

- **Elizabeth**, his wife, born about 1762 at **Faversham, Kent, England**; died
  1-10-1853 at Gundaroo aged 91. Agrees with the chart.
- **Hannah Guise**, born May 1793, died 24 May 1793 at Sydney — their first
  child, on the database but not on the chart. Added, flagged.
- **William of Bywong**: the chart says b. 25-5-1796, the database says
  27 July 1797. Both agree on his death, 17-3-1850 at Liverpool.

## Names as recorded

Everyone is entered under the surname of their own line, which is why the tree
reads Guise → Brownlow → Bradney → Jones → Rock, with Freestone, Staber and
Bastock marrying in. Mary Ann is the one exception: she is entered under her
married name with "née Guise" beneath, because that is the name she is known by.
